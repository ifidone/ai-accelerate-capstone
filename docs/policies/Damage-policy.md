# Damage & Loss Policy

## Reporting Damage

If equipment is damaged while checked out — a cracked probe, a bricked dev
board, a snapped connector — the student must report it before returning the
item, rather than returning it silently and hoping it isn't noticed. Report
by describing the damage to LabBot or by emailing the lab manager directly.
Reporting promptly, even for minor damage, is never held against a student;
what damages trust is discovering unreported damage during a later
inspection.

## What Happens After a Report

Once damage is reported, the item's status is changed to `under_repair`
rather than `available`, so it cannot be checked out by the next student.
The lab manager inspects the item and decides whether it needs a full
replacement, a minor repair, or is still usable with a noted defect (for
example, a scope probe that works but has a loose connector). This decision
determines whether the item returns to the available pool or is retired.

## Fees

Whether a fee applies depends on the cause, not just the outcome. Normal
wear and tear from legitimate coursework use is not billed. Damage from
clear misuse (dropping equipment, exceeding rated voltage, physical abuse)
may result in a repair or replacement fee charged to the student, at the lab
manager's discretion. There is no fixed fee schedule — the lab manager
evaluates each case individually and can be asked to explain their reasoning
for a given assessment.

## Loss

If an item cannot be found at return time, it is marked `lost` rather than
`overdue` once the student confirms they no longer have it. Lost items are
treated more seriously than damage, since they cannot be repaired and
directly reduce the pool available to other students. A lost oscilloscope or
similarly high-value item is escalated to the course instructor
automatically; a lost low-cost item (like a jumper wire kit) generally is
not.
